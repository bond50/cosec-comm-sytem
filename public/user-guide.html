User Guide

Using the COSEC Correspondence & Documents System

COSEC keeps track of every letter, memo, and file that comes into or goes out of the county office — who received it, who is handling it, what was decided, and when it was closed. This guide walks through the whole system with real example data, so you can follow along in the live app. Anything shown as a highlighted chip is example text you can click to copy, then paste into the matching field.

Workspace County Government of Vihiga
Central Office Office of the County Secretary and Head of Public Service
Who this is for Everyone using the system day to day
What it covers Every module, start to finish, with worked examples

How To Use This Guide

Four things worth knowing before you dive in.

1

One shared workspace

There is a single county workspace. You never need to "create an organization" — signing up simply adds you to it.

2

Your role decides what you see

The sidebar only shows the pages your role is allowed to use. If a section here is missing for you, that's expected, not a fault — see Understanding Your Role.

3

Example data is fictional

Every name, office, and note used as an example below is fictional, meant only to show you what a real entry looks like. Use your own real data day to day.

4

Every action is recorded

Every step a correspondence goes through — who touched it and when — is kept automatically, so nothing gets lost in between offices.

1. What COSEC Covers

COSEC follows the county's correspondence from first contact to final close-out.

Foundation

Signing in, departments, members, roles, and what each role can and can't do.

Business flow

Correspondence registration, review, routing, dispatch, acknowledgement, feedback, closure, and reopening.

Supporting modules

Documents, meetings, action points, notifications, search, dashboard, and reports.

2. Signing In

Creating your account

Open correspondences.vihiga.go.ke and choose to register. Enter your full name, work email address, and create a password (or continue with your Google account, if offered).

Expect the following the first time:

  • A Turnstile bot-check widget appears under the form — complete it before submitting.
  • You'll be asked to confirm your email — open the link sent to your inbox to activate your account.
  • You are automatically added to the county workspace — there is nothing to set up on your side.
  • Your account starts with basic access only. An administrator still needs to place you in your department and give you the right role (see Members & Roles) before you can register, review, or dispatch correspondence.

Signing in afterwards

Return to the login page, enter your email and password. Senior roles (administrators, the County Secretary/reviewing role) are asked for an extra one-time code sent to their email right after password sign-in, before the dashboard opens — this is a required security step, not an error, and it can't be skipped by navigating straight to the dashboard.

Forgot your password?

Use the "Forgot password" link on the login page and enter your email. If an account exists for that email, you'll receive a link to set a new password. The confirmation message is the same either way, so this never reveals whether an email has an account.

If an administrator creates your account for you directly (see Members & Roles) instead of you self- registering, you'll be given a temporary password and forced onto a "Set your password" screen the moment you first sign in.

3. Understanding Your Role

What you can do in COSEC depends on the role your administrator has given you.

Role What they typically do
Registry Officer Receives incoming mail and registers it into the system as the first step of the workflow.
Reviewing Officer / County Secretary Reviews newly registered correspondence, gives instructions, routes it to the right department, issues dispatch, and closes it once feedback is accepted.
Department User Works within one department: acknowledges correspondence and action points assigned to that department, and submits feedback once action is taken.
Management Broader oversight of correspondence and reporting, usually alongside a reviewing role.
Organization Admin Manages departments, members, and roles for the whole workspace.
Regular user Limited, view-focused access until an administrator assigns a fuller role.
A reserved SUPER_ADMIN role also exists for emergency recovery, but it can only be set directly in the database — you won't see it as an option anywhere in the app, and that's by design.

If you're not sure what your own role can do, open Roles & access from the sidebar (if visible to you) — see How Access Works below.

4. Departments

The county's structure is one central office plus any number of execution departments. This was already set up when the workspace was created; this section is for the ongoing admin work of keeping it current.

The central office

Every workspace has exactly one central office, created automatically when the organization was first set up:

Name Office of the County Secretary and Head of Public Service
Code OCS-HPS
Kind Central office
Description Central control office for registration, review, dispatch, oversight, and closure.

Adding an execution department

Open People & structure → Manage entities and use "Add entity". For example:

Name Code Kind
Department of Health Services HLT Execution unit
Department of Transport and Infrastructure TRN Execution unit
Department of Finance and Economic Planning FIN Execution unit

Execution departments become available as routing destinations for correspondence and as assignment targets for action points as soon as they're saved.

Deactivating a department (instead of deleting it) is the normal way to retire one — its history stays intact, but it stops appearing as a routing choice for new correspondence.

5. Members & Roles

Adding a member

Open People & structure → Members and click "Add member" (or go straight to /dashboard/members?create=1). This sets a working password directly and marks the email pre-verified — no activation email needed. For example:

User Department Roles
Registry Officer Office of the County Secretary and Head of Public Service REGISTRY_OFFICER
Reviewing Officer / County Secretary Office of the County Secretary and Head of Public Service REVIEWING_OFFICER + MANAGEMENT
Health Officer Department of Health Services DEPARTMENT_USER
Transport Officer Department of Transport and Infrastructure DEPARTMENT_USER

Rules the form enforces for you:

  • Central-office roles (REGISTRY_OFFICER, REVIEWING_OFFICER) only appear when the central office department is selected.
  • Execution departments only offer execution-appropriate roles such as DEPARTMENT_USER.
  • Department is mandatory for an active member.
Admin-set passwords can be short (like 1234) since they're temporary and handed off directly — the new member is forced onto a "Set your password" screen the first time they sign in, before reaching the dashboard.
Double-check spelling as you type a member's name — it shows up everywhere that account is displayed later: movement history, meeting organizer/chairperson, action point assignee.

Editing a member

Open a member's detail page to change their name, email, roles, department, or status (Active / Suspended / Inactive). The system will refuse to let the workspace's last active ORG_ADMIN be demoted or suspended — assign ORG_ADMIN to someone else first if that's genuinely what you need to do.

Resetting your own password

From /auth/reset, enter your email to receive a reset link. Opening it lets you set a new password immediately; the old one stops working right away.

6. How Access Works

The mandatory code step for admin roles

Signing in as an ORG_ADMIN (or another admin-level role) sends you to a one-time code challenge right after your password is accepted — the dashboard is not reachable until that code is entered, even by navigating directly to /dashboard in a new tab.

Wrong-destination blocking

Roles outside the central office (for example a DEPARTMENT_USER) cannot open member management, access policy, or the correspondence registration page — those routes redirect away or are blocked outright rather than exposing controls the role isn't meant to use. The same accounts can freely open the dashboard, correspondence list, documents, and meetings, just with management controls hidden.

The Access page

If your role allows it, open Administration → Roles & access for a plain- language role guide: pick any role in the filter list to see its summary, placement, and responsibilities, which pages and actions it unlocks, and the underlying page/action permission table. It's the same source of truth the app itself enforces, so what it shows always matches what that role can actually do.

7. Correspondence Lifecycle

This is the core of COSEC: every piece of incoming mail moves through the same journey, and the system keeps a full movement and audit record at every stage.

Receive → Register → Review → Dispatch → Action → Feedback → Close (with Reopen available if more work turns out to be needed)

Registering incoming mail (Registry Officer)

Open Correspondence → Register. This form is incoming-only — every record registered here is automatically type Incoming.

The first field after the received date is Source type — a two-button choice between Internal county office and External sender. For an external letter, use data like this:

Received date and time Now
Source type External sender
Subject Request for road repair status update
Description Requesting a status update on road repair works in Vihiga.
Sender name Jane Atieno
Sender office Office of the Governor
Delivered by John Courier
Registry receiver Leave as "Use current signed-in user" — this is who the record's "received by" is set to.
Delivery method Hand delivery
Priority Urgent
Confidentiality Internal

Once saved:

  • A reference number is generated automatically, for example VCG/OCS/2026/04/000001.
  • Status starts as NEW.
  • Received by is set to the Registry Officer who registered it.
  • A first audit entry and first movement entry are created.
  • The detail page's "Source" field reads Office of the Governor · External sender.
Choosing Internal county office instead lets you pick an executing department from a dropdown rather than typing a sender office by hand — the "Source" field then reads something like Department of Transport and Infrastructure (TRN) · Internal county office, a real structured link to that department rather than free text.

Reviewing and instructing (Reviewing Officer / County Secretary)

Open the newly registered item from Correspondence → All correspondence and add instructions, for example: 

Please verify road repair progress and return feedback with current status.

Saving this moves status to UNDER_REVIEW, records the reviewer's identity, and adds the instructions to the review block.

Routing (Reviewing Officer / County Secretary)

Decide who should act on it, for example:

  • Destination department: Department of Transport and Infrastructure
  • Receiving officer: Transport Officer
  • Due date: a near-future date

This makes the assigned department/user and due date visible on the record and moves it toward dispatch.

Dispatching

Issue the dispatch with a short note, for example:

Kindly act and submit feedback before the due date.

This formally hands the correspondence to the receiving department, moves status to DISPATCHED, and starts the due-date clock. The receiving side becomes the accountable next actor.

Acknowledging and acting (Department User)

When something is dispatched to your department, you'll see Confirm receipt on its detail page (review, routing, and close controls stay hidden from you). Clicking it records the acknowledgement timestamp and moves status to IN_PROGRESS.

Once your department has taken action, submit feedback, for example: 

Road repair progress verified. Works are ongoing and the department will submit the final completion report after site confirmation.

This stores the feedback timestamp and note and moves status to AWAITING_FEEDBACK.

Closing (Reviewing Officer / County Secretary)

Close controls appear only once feedback exists. Closing with a note, for example: 

Feedback reviewed and accepted. Correspondence closed after Transport Department response.

moves status to CLOSED. The only control left after that is a deliberately amber-colored Reopen button, styled differently so it isn't clicked by accident.

Reopening for another round

If more work turns out to be needed, reopen with a required justification, for example: 

Additional clarification requested after closure. Reopening for follow-up review and routing.

Only the closure-authority role sees this control. Status returns to UNDER_REVIEW, and you can route and dispatch again — the previous cycle's history stays fully visible alongside the new one, so nothing from the first round is lost.

Every one of these steps — registration, review, routing, dispatch, acknowledgement, feedback, closure, reopening — is kept on the record's movement history, so anyone can see exactly who handled it and when.

8. Document Registry

Open Documents from the sidebar to register physical or digital files separately from correspondence — for example a physical file that needs to be tracked by holder and location.

Categories

Click "Manage categories" to organize documents, for example into Official Letters, Internal Memos, and Reports and Returns. Each category shows a document count; a category still holding documents can't be deleted until they're moved or removed, so nothing is silently orphaned.

Creating a document

Like correspondence, every document has an Origin choice between Internal county office and External source. For example, an external physical file:

Title Physical file for road repair request
Category Official Letters
Medium Physical
Origin External source
Origin office Office of the Governor
Brought by John Courier
Current holder Reviewing Officer / County Secretary
Current location OCS-HPS Registry Cabinet A
Status Received

A reference number is generated automatically. You can also link a document to the correspondence it relates to — it then shows up under that correspondence's own "Linked documents" section too.

Uploads and archiving

Upload supporting files to a document; they open in a new tab. Archiving a document keeps it searchable and readable but makes it read-only. Editing a document lets you update location, holder, and status at any time, including switching the Origin between Internal and External.

If a PDF specifically fails to load while other file types open fine, that's a known Cloudinary account setting ("Restricted media types"), not something wrong with your upload — mention it to an administrator rather than re-uploading repeatedly.

Department users typically get view-only access to documents — create, edit, upload, archive, and "Manage categories" stay available to registry/reviewing/management/admin roles.

9. Meetings & Action Points

Scheduling a meeting

Create a meeting with its agenda, venue, and time, for example:

Title Monthly departmental heads review
Agenda 1. Correspondence backlog 2. Road repair follow-up 3. AOB
Venue County Secretary's Boardroom
Organizer / Chairperson Reviewing Officer / County Secretary

Action points

From the meeting detail page, create action points and assign each to a person and/or department with a due date, for example:

  • Follow up with Transport dept on road repair correspondence status, assigned to a Transport department user and the Department of Transport and Infrastructure, due in 5 days.

The meeting's own status automatically advances to Action points issued the first time an action point is added.

Updating your own action point: if an action point is assigned to you or your department, open the meeting and use the update box under it to change its status and add remarks — this works even without a manager role. Only reassigning it to someone else, changing its department, or moving its due date stays manager-only.

Every status change and remark is kept in a "Change history" panel under the action point, with who made the change and when.

Your own assignments across every meeting are collected under My action points in the sidebar.

Holding and closing a meeting

Once the meeting happens, edit it to set Status to Held and record a discussion summary and resolutions. Closing a meeting requires recorded resolutions or at least one action point — the form will tell you if that's missing.

Printing and linking

"Print meeting summary" opens a clean, standalone page (agenda, discussion, resolutions, decisions, and action points) with a Print button that doesn't itself appear in the printed output. A meeting can also be linked to a specific correspondence, showing up on both records so the two stay connected.

10. Notifications

The bell icon in the top header alerts you whenever something needs your attention:

Event Who's notified
Correspondence awaiting review Review authority only
Dispatch issued Direct receiving user first, or department fallback if no user is assigned
Feedback returned Review authority only
Closed / Cancelled Execution side plus central-office visibility
Action point assigned Direct assignee first, department fallback if no direct user

Click a notification to jump straight to the related record. "Mark all as read" clears your unread badges, and an unread-only filter is available in the notification workspace.

11. Search

Use the search bar in the top header. It understands both keywords and short intents, for example:

  • transport
  • urgent
  • add member
  • documents archived
  • my action points

Results and quick actions are scoped to what you're allowed to see — a department-level role searching correspondence only gets hits for records assigned to them or their department, not the whole county register.

12. Dashboard & Reports

Dashboard

Your dashboard summarizes what needs attention right now — urgent items, pending dispatches, and recent activity, all clickable. Registry, reviewing, management, and admin roles see the full county picture; a department-level role's dashboard is scoped to their own department and assignments.

A tile reading "0" isn't automatically wrong — each one only counts records that currently match its exact condition (for example, "Pending dispatch" only counts correspondence genuinely mid- dispatch right now). If every item you're tracking has since been closed, several tiles legitimately read 0.

Reports

Open Reports from the sidebar (visible to admin/reviewing/management roles) for county-wide SLA, workload, and trend analysis: summary cards for pending review, pending acknowledgement, and overdue action points; status/priority/ meeting-status breakdown charts; a six-month intake trend; and an export button for a print-ready CSV summary.

13. Getting Help

If something in COSEC isn't behaving as this guide describes, or you're missing access you believe you should have, contact your organization administrator — they can check or adjust your role and department from People & structure, or escalate further if it turns out to be a system issue.
