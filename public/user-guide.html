Using the COSEC Correspondence & Documents System

COSEC keeps track of every letter, memo, and file that comes into or goes out of the county office — who received it, who is handling it, what was decided, and when it was closed. This guide walks you through using the system day to day. No technical background is needed.

Before You Start

Four things worth knowing before you dive in.

1

One shared workspace

There is a single county workspace. You never need to "create an organization" — signing up simply adds you to it.

2

Your role decides what you see

The menu on the left only shows the pages your role is allowed to use. If something described here is missing for you, that is expected, not a fault.

3

An administrator sets you up

After you sign up, an administrator assigns your department and role. Until that happens you may see limited options.

4

Every action is recorded

Every step a correspondence goes through — who touched it and when — is kept automatically, so nothing gets lost in between offices.

1. Signing In

Creating your account

Open the COSEC website and choose to register. Enter your work email address and create a password (or continue with your Google account, if that option is offered). You'll be asked to confirm your email — open the link sent to your inbox to activate your account.

Expect the following the first time:

  • You are automatically added to the county workspace — there is nothing to set up on your side.
  • Your account starts with basic access only. An administrator still needs to place you in your department and give you the right role before you can register, review, or dispatch correspondence.

Signing in afterwards

Return to the login page, enter your email and password. Senior roles (administrators, the County Secretary/reviewing role) are asked for an extra one-time code sent to their email — this is a security step, not an error.

Forgot your password?

Use the "Forgot password" link on the login page and enter your email. If an account exists for that email, you'll receive a link to set a new password.

The first time an administrator creates your account for you directly (instead of you self-registering), you'll be given a temporary password and asked to set your own the moment you first sign in.

2. Understanding Your Role

What you can do in COSEC depends on the role your administrator has given you.

Role What they typically do
Registry Officer Receives incoming mail and registers it into the system as the first step of the workflow.
Reviewing Officer / County Secretary Reviews newly registered correspondence, gives instructions, routes it to the right department, issues dispatch, and closes it once feedback is accepted.
Department User Works within one department: acknowledges correspondence dispatched to that department and submits feedback once action is taken.
Management Broader oversight of correspondence and reporting, usually alongside a reviewing role.
Organization Admin Manages departments, members, and roles for the whole workspace.
Regular user Limited, view-focused access until an administrator assigns a fuller role.

If you're not sure what your role can do, open Roles & access from the sidebar (if visible to you) — it explains, in plain terms, what each role is meant for and exactly which pages and actions it unlocks.

3. Around The Dashboard

Once signed in, three things stay on screen wherever you go:

Left sidebar

Your navigation menu. Only shows the areas your role is allowed to use — Dashboard, Correspondence, Documents, Meetings, and, for administrators, People & structure.

Top header

Search bar, the notification bell, and your account menu (name, organization, sign out).

Dashboard home

A summary of what needs attention right now — urgent items, pending dispatches, and recent activity, all clickable.

A number reading "0" on the dashboard isn't necessarily wrong — a tile only counts records that currently match its exact condition (for example, "Pending dispatch" only counts correspondence that is genuinely mid-dispatch right now).

4. Handling Correspondence

This is the core of COSEC: every piece of incoming mail moves through the same journey, and the system keeps a full record at every stage.

Receive → Register → Review → Dispatch → Action → Feedback → Close

Registering incoming mail (Registry Officer)

Open Correspondence → Register. Fill in the sender's details, subject, description, how it was delivered, and how urgent and confidential it is. If it came from within the county government, choose "Internal county office" and pick the sending department; if it came from outside, choose "External sender" and type in the sender's office.

Once saved, the system gives it a reference number automatically — this is how it will be tracked and referred to from now on.

Reviewing and instructing (Reviewing Officer / County Secretary)

Open the newly registered item from Correspondence → All correspondence. Add any instructions for how it should be handled, then decide which department and officer should act on it, and by when.

Dispatching

Once routing is decided, issue the dispatch with a short note. This formally hands the correspondence to the receiving department and starts their due date.

Acknowledging and acting (Department User)

When something is dispatched to your department, you'll see Confirm receipt on its detail page — click it to acknowledge you've received it. Once your department has taken action, submit your feedback describing what was done.

Closing (Reviewing Officer / County Secretary)

Once feedback has been submitted and reviewed, close the correspondence. If more work turns out to be needed later, an amber-coloured Reopen button lets an authorized reviewer send it through another round.

Every one of these steps — registration, review, dispatch, acknowledgement, feedback, closure — is kept on the record's movement history, so anyone can see exactly who handled it and when.

5. Documents

Open Documents from the sidebar to register physical or digital files separately from correspondence — for example a physical file that needs to be tracked by location and holder. Group documents into categories, link a document to the correspondence it relates to, upload a scanned copy, and archive a document once it's no longer active. Archived documents stay searchable but become read-only.

6. Meetings & Action Points

Open Meetings to schedule a meeting with its agenda, venue, and time. After the meeting is held, record the discussion summary and resolutions. Action points can be created under a meeting and assigned to a person or department with a due date — assigned staff can then update their own action points' status from My action points in the sidebar. A meeting can also be linked to a specific correspondence so the two stay connected.

7. Notifications

The bell icon in the top header alerts you whenever something needs your attention — a correspondence awaiting your review, a dispatch sent to your department, feedback returned, or an action point assigned to you. Click a notification to jump straight to the related item, and use "Mark all as read" to clear your list.

8. Searching

Use the search bar in the top header to find correspondence, documents, members, or pages by typing a subject, a name, a department, or a status like "urgent". Search only shows results you're allowed to see.

9. Reports

Open Reports from the sidebar for a summary view: how much correspondence is pending review, pending acknowledgement, or overdue, broken down by status, priority, and department, plus a monthly intake trend. Use the export button to download a print-ready summary.

10. Getting Help

If something in COSEC isn't behaving as this guide describes, or you're missing access you believe you should have, contact your organization administrator — they can check or adjust your role and department from People & structure, or escalate it further if it turns out to be a system issue.
